# CLAUDE.md

## Project
This repository contains the personal literary website of Acacio Barros, focused primarily on poetry.
This site is distinct from my academic website and professional identity as a professor and researcher. Do not import assumptions, language, visual conventions, biographical emphasis, or content from my academic website unless I explicitly request it.
The purpose of this site is to present my poetry and literary work with clarity, restraint, and seriousness.

## Identity and Persona
Treat Acacio Barros the poet as a distinct public-facing persona.
This does not mean inventing a fictional biography or concealing factual information. It means that the literary site should foreground the poetic work and the aspects of my identity relevant to it rather than my academic credentials.
The site should primarily present me as a poet. Do not foreground my academic identity automatically. The approved biography identifies me as a physicist and philosopher; that wording is a deliberate editorial decision and is to be preserved as written. Beyond it, do not add academic credentials, institutional affiliations, professional titles, or professional biography unless I explicitly request them.
Do not introduce academic terminology, intellectual credentials, institutional affiliations, or scholarly accomplishments unless I specifically decide they belong on this site.
The literary persona should emerge primarily through:

* the poems;
* the selection and organization of the work;
* a concise literary biography;
* the visual and editorial character of the website.

Avoid self-mythologizing, grand claims, or promotional language.

## Editorial Principles
Preserve my voice.
When editing poetry:

* Do not rewrite a poem merely to make it smoother or more conventional.
* Do not normalize unusual syntax, punctuation, spacing, capitalization, repetition, ambiguity, or diction without asking.
* Treat apparent irregularities as potentially intentional.
* Distinguish typographical errors from poetic choices.
* When proposing substantive changes, explain the reason and show the original and proposed wording.
* Prefer suggestions over silent alteration.

Do not imitate another poet's style.
When editing prose about the poetry, use clear, precise, understated language. Avoid generic literary language such as:

* "explores themes of"
* "delves into"
* "evocative"
* "poignant"
* "powerful"
* "timeless"
* "thought-provoking"
unless the wording is genuinely necessary and specific.

Avoid explaining what the poems "mean" unless I explicitly ask for interpretation.

## Website Aesthetic
The site should be visually restrained and text-centered.
Prefer:

* generous whitespace;
* excellent typography;
* simple navigation;
* readable line lengths;
* strong mobile presentation;
* minimal visual distraction;
* static HTML and CSS where practical.

Avoid:

* unnecessary JavaScript;
* animation for its own sake;
* elaborate UI effects;
* decorative literary clichés;
* manuscript, parchment, quill, typewriter, or similar motifs unless explicitly requested;
* excessive ornamentation;
* anything that competes with the poems.

The poetry itself should remain the principal visual object.

## Typography and Poem Formatting
Poetic formatting is content, not decoration.
Preserve:

* line breaks;
* stanza breaks;
* indentation;
* deliberate blank space;
* capitalization;
* punctuation.

Never allow responsive CSS to unintentionally alter the structure of a poem.
If a poem is too wide for a small screen, identify the problem rather than silently reflowing lines.
Use semantic HTML where possible.
Accessibility should be maintained without compromising intentional poetic structure.

## Architecture
Keep the site technically simple unless there is a clear reason not to.
Prefer:

* static HTML;
* CSS;
* minimal dependencies;
* GitHub Pages compatibility;
* straightforward directory structures;
* human-readable source files.

Do not introduce frameworks, build systems, package managers, templating engines, or JavaScript libraries merely because they are common modern practice.
Before adding a dependency, explain what problem it solves and whether the same result can reasonably be achieved without it.

## Content Organization
Do not assume that poems should be organized chronologically.
Possible structures may include:

* individual poems;
* sequences or cycles;
* collections;
* published and unpublished work;
* translations;
* recordings;
* selected poems.

Recommend organizational changes only when they improve the reader's encounter with the work.
Avoid turning the site into a résumé or bibliography unless I request those features.

## Biography
The biography should be short unless I explicitly request a longer version.
Do not fabricate literary publications, awards, affiliations, influences, places, experiences, or biographical details.
Do not infer biographical facts from poems.
Academic information should be included only deliberately and in proportion to its relevance to the literary identity presented here.

## Tone
For site prose, prefer language that is:

* restrained;
* precise;
* literate;
* direct;
* unpretentious.

Avoid marketing language, exaggerated claims, and generic artistic self-description.
Do not describe the work as important, profound, original, moving, or significant on my behalf.
Let readers make those judgments.

## Working Method
Before making significant changes:

1. Inspect the existing files and understand the current structure.
2. Preserve working behavior unless a change is intentional.
3. Make the smallest coherent change that accomplishes the goal.
4. Do not rewrite unrelated files.
5. Explain material design or architectural decisions.
6. Check desktop and mobile implications.
7. Preserve the exact text and formatting of poems unless explicitly authorized to edit them.

When my request is ambiguous in a way that could materially alter a poem, public biography, site architecture, or visual identity, ask before making that decision.

## Relationship to Other Projects
This repository is independent of my academic website.
Do not copy design, prose, metadata, biographies, navigation, or content from other repositories associated with me unless I explicitly request it.
Similarity between the sites should arise only where I deliberately choose it.

## Governing Principle
The website exists to give the poems an appropriate place to be read.
When choosing between a technically impressive solution and a quieter solution that serves the poetry better, prefer the quieter solution.
